# BuildExchange ZM: four defensible business answers

These are testable proposals for a student venture, not claims of proven demand, partnerships, investment or profitability. The implemented local demo demonstrates software workflows, not market traction.

## 1. Differentiation and business strategy

Focus first on small building contractors and site owners who need to reconcile construction purchases against what reaches the site. The proposed advantage is a linked record of supplier quotation, ordered material quantities, transporter custody updates and a supervisor's quantity-and-condition inspection. A delivery marked “arrived” is deliberately different from a site receipt; shortages remain visible as discrepancies instead of being hidden by a delivery tick.

General equipment rentals add timed availability, deposit bookkeeping and an inspected return record. Equipment includes generators, pumps and power tools, not only construction machinery. The aim is to reduce coordination and evidence gaps across procurement, transport and site inspection. This is a workflow hypothesis, not a verified claim that competing services lack these functions. Do not claim superiority over Yango without a current, documented competitor study.

Proposed revenue experiments: a transparent fee on completed procurement orders, equipment booking fees, and optional contractor record-management subscriptions. Negotiate transport separately during the pilot. The prototype now demonstrates a 5% service-fee calculation; that is an illustrative rate, not validated revenue. Subscription billing and commercial profit calculation remain unimplemented. Validate willingness to pay and support costs before choosing rates.

## 2. Go-to-market and sales

Begin with one manageable service area and a small consenting pilot cohort. Recruit hardware retailers, equipment owners, independent transporters and contractor/site-supervisor pairs through direct visits and demonstrations. Sell the practical outcome: “Compare supply, arrange transport, and keep a signed-in inspection record for what arrived.”

Use assisted onboarding, published stock/pricing responsibilities and a short demonstration of a shortage case. Ask contractors to trial one real procurement journey only after operational, privacy and dispute procedures are ready. Compare their current paper process with the digital record. Convert repeat users through demonstrated reliability and transparent terms; do not depend on broad consumer advertising at the start.

## 3. Incremental market value

The proposed value is traceability across the handoff between supplier, transporter and site supervisor: price snapshots, separate payment records, ordered-versus-received counts, photo evidence and actor/time event histories. Multi-retailer discovery and timed equipment access support that core chain of custody.

Measure whether this reduces reconciliation time, missing documentation and unresolved discrepancies. The system records evidence; it does not prove that all reports are truthful or automatically determine liability. Photos are re-encoded to remove metadata and hashed to identify the stored version. The receipt is authenticated by account access, not a qualified legal electronic signature.

## 4. Investor attraction

Prepare a demonstrable product and a consented pilot evidence pack before fundraising. Seek introductions through contractor networks, retailer/equipment-owner relationships, university enterprise programmes and suitable local enterprise or angel opportunities after verifying their current eligibility. Pursue letters of intent and pilot agreements; never represent informal interest as a signed partnership.

Proposed pilot measurements (no results yet):

| Measurement | Definition / evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Site reconciliation time | Inspection completion timestamp minus arrival timestamp; compare with a documented paper-process baseline |
| Quantity discrepancy frequency | Receipts with a shortage divided by all inspected receipts in the same cohort |
| Discrepancy resolution time | Recorded resolution minus first reported discrepancy; resolution workflow is a future feature |
| Repeat use | Contractors placing a subsequent completed order within a stated observation window |
| Rental utilization | Booked unit-hours divided by available unit-hours in the stated period |
| Completion rate | Receipted orders divided by orders placed in a defined creation cohort; report rejections separately |
| Unit economics | Actual fee income minus transport subsidies, payment charges, refunds, verification and support costs; exclude supplier pass-through value |

Provide consented interview notes, real cohort sizes, dated records, retention and cost evidence. Use a funding ask tied to specific next milestones such as a controlled pilot, independently checked security, and tested hosting. No invented percentages, market sizes, investor commitments or customer counts are supplied here.
